# Land for Sale Near Panama City Beach and Destin: Why Smart Buyers Look an Hour Inland

Northwest Florida's Emerald Coast is one of the most beautiful stretches of shoreline in the country. Sugar-white sand, green-blue Gulf water, and towns like Panama City Beach, Destin, and Seaside draw millions of visitors a year. So it's no surprise that "land for sale near Panama City Beach" and "land for sale near Destin" are some of the most common searches we see from buyers.

The trouble is what those searches usually turn up: small lots, high prices per square foot, HOA rules, and parcels that sit in flood or storm surge zones. For many buyers, the better move is to look about an hour inland, where the same beaches are still a day trip away but the land comes in acres instead of fractions of one.

Here's how that tradeoff works, what you gain and give up, and what to check when you compare a coastal lot with inland acreage.

## Why Land Near the Beach Is So Hard to Buy

Coastal land in the Florida Panhandle is limited by geography. There's only so much ground between the Gulf, the bays, and the protected state and federal lands along the coast, and much of it was developed years ago. What's left tends to share a few traits:

- **Small parcels.** Near the beach, a "large" lot is often a quarter acre or less. Finding 5 or 10 acres near the coast is rare, and very expensive when it turns up.
- **Prices set by vacation rental demand.** Coastal land often competes with investors planning short-term rentals, which pushes prices well above what the dirt alone would be worth.
- **More rules.** Coastal subdivisions commonly come with HOA dues, architectural review, and restrictions on outbuildings, fencing, animals, and what you can park on the property.
- **Extra coastal regulation.** Florida regulates construction near the shoreline more strictly. Building seaward of the state's Coastal Construction Control Line requires a permit from the Florida Department of Environmental Protection, and coastal flood zones can affect how high and how you build.
- **Higher carrying costs.** Property taxes follow value, and insurance near the water can cost a lot more than inland coverage.

None of that means coastal property is a bad buy. It just means you pay a premium for being steps from the sand, and many buyers don't need to be steps away. They need to be *close enough*.

## The "Hour From the Beach" Sweet Spot

For most people, the beach is a place they visit on a Saturday, not a place they need to see from the kitchen window. Once you accept a drive of an hour or so, the choices open up a lot.

Inland Northwest Florida, in places like Jackson, Washington, and Holmes counties, is rural, wooded, and full of springs and rivers. Land here is commonly sold in 5, 10, and 20+ acre tracts, often at a price that would buy a small fraction of an acre near the Gulf.

Take Marianna, the county seat of Jackson County, as an example. Approximate drive times from the Marianna area:

- **Panama City Beach:** roughly 1 to 1.5 hours, heading south on US-231 or SR-77
- **Destin and the 30A beaches:** roughly 1.5 to 2 hours, west on I-10
- **Northwest Florida Beaches International Airport (ECP):** about 1 hour
- **Tallahassee:** about 1 hour east on I-10
- **Dothan, Alabama:** about 45 minutes north for shopping and medical care

Times depend on exactly where you start and on summer beach traffic, so map the route from any parcel you're considering. But the point holds: you can leave home after breakfast, spend the day on the Gulf, and be back on your own land for dinner.

## What You Gain by Buying Inland

### Real acreage

This is the big one. Instead of a lot that fits a house and a driveway, you get room for a home, a barn, a garden, a shop, trails, a pond site, or simply woods between you and the nearest neighbor. ### Privacy and quiet

Beach towns are busy, especially from spring break through Labor Day. Inland, the traffic is a tractor on a county road. Many buyers tell us that the quiet, the dark night sky, and the wildlife are what sold them, more than the price.

### Fewer restrictions on how you use the land

Rural parcels outside of HOA communities often allow uses coastal subdivisions don't, like horses and livestock, agricultural use, barns, RV or boat storage, and hunting on larger tracts. Rules vary by county and by any deed restrictions on the property, so always confirm the zoning and future land use designation with the county before you buy. ### Lower costs to hold the land

A lower purchase price usually means lower property taxes. Land with a bona fide agricultural use, such as cattle, hay, or timber, may also qualify for Florida's agricultural classification, which can lower taxes further. Inland parcels also typically sit outside the coastal high-risk flood zones that drive up insurance near the water.

### Freshwater recreation the coast doesn't have

The Gulf gets the attention, but inland North Florida has its own water: crystal-clear springs, spring-fed rivers, and lakes. Around Marianna alone you'll find Blue Springs Recreation Area, Merritt's Mill Pond, the Chipola River, and Florida Caverns State Park. That gives you a beach day on the weekend and a spring swim on a Tuesday evening. ## An Honest Word About Hurricanes

You'll sometimes hear that buying inland means you don't have to think about hurricanes. That isn't true, and anyone buying land in the Panhandle should know it.

Moving inland mostly protects you from **storm surge**, the coastal flooding that causes much of the worst damage along the shoreline. It does not protect you from **wind**. When Hurricane Michael came ashore near Mexico Beach in 2018, it was still a powerful storm when it reached Jackson County, about 70 miles inland, and it caused serious damage in and around Marianna.

What that means in practice:

- Build to Florida's building code, which is designed with hurricane winds in mind.
- Place your homesite on high, well-drained ground, and check the parcel's FEMA flood zone.
- Think about trees near the house. Large pines and oaks are beautiful, but keep a safe distance from where you build.
- Get an insurance quote early so you know the real cost of owning a home there.

Handled that way, inland acreage gives you a lot less exposure than a beachfront lot, without pretending the risk is zero.

Over the years we've noticed a few types of buyers who are especially happy with this tradeoff:

- **Families who want a weekend place.** A cabin or RV pad on acreage, with the beach and springs nearby, makes for an easy getaway, and the land stays in the family for years.
- **Future retirees.** Many buyers purchase now and build later, locking in the land while they still have their pick. We wrote about this in [Buying Your Forever Land in Florida](/blog/buying-your-forever-land-in-florida-why-more-families-are-purchasing-before-retirement).

- **Outdoor people.** Hunters, paddlers, divers, and anglers get woods, springs, rivers, and the Gulf all within reach.
- **Long-term land investors.** Rural land in a region with steady population growth is a straightforward, low-maintenance asset to hold.

## Questions to Ask When Comparing a Coastal Lot to Inland Acreage

If you're weighing a lot near the beach against a larger tract inland, compare them on the same terms:

1. **What is the price per acre**, not just the total price?
2. **What can I build or do here?** Check zoning, future land use, HOA rules, and deed restrictions.
3. **What's the flood zone**, and what will insurance cost for the home I want to build?
4. **What does it cost to get the land ready?** On rural land, ask about power, a well, septic, clearing, and a driveway.
5. **What are the annual property taxes**, and does the land qualify for an agricultural classification?
6. **How long is the drive** to the beach, groceries, a hospital, and an airport?
7. **Is the title clean and the land surveyed?** Make sure you know exactly what you're buying.

Once you run the numbers this way, many buyers find that an inland parcel gives them far more land and freedom for the money, even after budgeting for well, septic, and power.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you still find land for sale near Panama City Beach?

Yes, but near the beach it's usually small lots at a premium. Buyers who want acreage typically look north of the coast, in rural Bay County or in neighboring inland counties like Washington and Jackson, where larger tracts are more common and prices per acre are much lower.

### How far is Marianna, FL from the beach?

Marianna is roughly 1 to 1.5 hours from Panama City Beach and about 1.5 to 2 hours from Destin, depending on where you start and on traffic.

### Is inland Florida Panhandle land safe from hurricanes?

No location in the Panhandle is free of hurricane risk. Inland land is largely out of reach of storm surge, which is a major source of coastal damage, but strong winds can still reach well inland. Building to code, choosing high ground, and getting insurance quotes early are the best ways to plan for it.
